About Me

Hi, I'm Amrahata Paalisbo.

I work as an SEO Specialist and Executive Assistant — my role is to take SEO tasks off a business owner's plate and make sure they actually get done. Day to day, that means keyword research, on-page optimization, publishing and updating content, local SEO updates, and reaching out for backlink opportunities.

My career actually started back in 2012 as a Virtual Assistant, with stops along the way as a Customer Service Representative and an HR Recruiter before I moved into SEO and digital marketing. That background is why the "Executive Assistant" part of my title isn't just a label — inbox and calendar management, scheduling, data entry, and day-to-day admin support are things I've genuinely done for years, and I'm comfortable picking them back up alongside SEO work.

I've supported HVAC, indoor air quality, healthcare, local business, and technology brands — usually working behind the scenes on WordPress, keeping content organized, and tracking performance through Google Search Console and Analytics so I can flag what's working and what needs attention.

I've also been paying closer attention to how AI tools like ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, and Google's AI Overviews are changing how people search, and I enjoy learning how that affects the everyday SEO work I do — like writing content that's easy for both readers and AI to understand.

If you need someone dependable to handle the SEO to-do list — research, content, on-page fixes, reporting, and the small recurring tasks that keep adding up — that's where I can help.

Case Study · Content Operations

WordPress SEO Content Publishing at Scale

How I supported a repeatable, quality-first WordPress publishing process for Filterbuy, a U.S. HVAC and air filtration company.

ClientFilterbuy Inc.
IndustryHVAC / Air Filtration / Home Services
Project period2023–2026
My roleSEO Specialist | WordPress Content Publishing & QA
~50 SEO content pieces per week

Supported as part of a team publishing workflow. This reflects a team pipeline, not 50 articles written or published independently by me.

Project overview

I supported a recurring team pipeline covering WordPress content optimization and publication, quality checks and ongoing SEO reviews. My responsibilities included SEO content briefs, publishing, formatting, metadata optimization, internal linking, quality assurance, existing-page reoptimization and SOP documentation.

The business challenge

Filterbuy needed a repeatable way to prepare, publish and review high volumes of search-focused content across WordPress sites without losing consistency in formatting, on-page SEO or editorial quality.

My role and responsibilities

1. Content planning & briefs

Keyword and intent inputs, page outlines, target headings and content structure.

2. WordPress publishing & SEO

Formatting, metadata, headings, image checks, internal links and page updates.

3. Editorial QA & processes

Draft checks, inconsistencies, link reviews, reoptimization and documented SOPs.

Content publishing workflow

The original client SOP used structured SEO briefs, Claude-supported drafts, brand voice checks, keyword-linked pages and editorial QA. This seven-step framework is a modernized illustration based on that SOP, not a claim that every improvement was implemented during the client engagement.

  1. Research & Search IntentConfirm the audience question, primary intent, existing coverage and page purpose.
  2. SEO BriefBuild H1/H2 structure, topical subquestions, relevant entities and suitable sources.
  3. AI-Assisted DraftingUse AI for scaffolding and editing; follow the brand guide and add real subject value.
  4. Human Fact-CheckingCheck claims, statistics, quotes, sources, dates and sensitive advice.
  5. On-Page OptimizationWrite useful metadata, natural anchors, context links and scannable sections.
  6. WordPress QAReview blocks, visuals, alt text, responsive layout, links, URL and publish settings.
  7. Publish & ImproveCheck the live page, then use Search Console insights for meaningful refreshes.

SEO optimization process & WordPress quality assurance

What a publish-ready page needs:

Content

Intent answered directly; non-generic value added; claims, quotes and sources verified.

Structure

One clear H1, descriptive H2/H3s, useful summaries, readable paragraphs and lists.

On-page SEO

Title and description checked; links relevant; keywords natural; no forced anchor quotas.

WordPress

Correct block styling, image alt text, responsive display, URL, categories and publish status.

Final review

Preview checked on mobile and desktop; links tested; approvals and corrections tracked.

Post-publish

Live page checked; revisit only when search data or changed facts justify updates.

AI-assisted content workflow

The 2026 editorial principle

Human value first. AI accelerates the workflow; it does not replace evidence, originality or final accountability.

Deliverables and operational impact

Verified from project history
  • SEO publishing and WordPress optimization
  • Content QA and reoptimization
  • SOP support
  • Support of a recurring ~50-piece/week team workflow
Not claimed
  • Individual authorship of all 50 pieces
  • A measured traffic lift or ranking gain
  • Revenue impact or AI citation gains from this specific process

Key takeaways

A process protects quality

A defined brief-to-QA flow keeps formatting and on-page SEO consistent at volume.

Check before publishing

Fact-checks, link reviews and WordPress QA catch issues before pages go live.

Documented and repeatable

SOPs let a team publish consistently and improve pages over time.

Scope note: The workflow is an updated example informed by the original SOP and current Google Search guidance. It describes work I performed and a process I would bring to similar projects; it is not a record of measured results.

Case Study · Independent Audit

WordPress Content QA & On-Page SEO Reoptimization

An independent review of a public Filterbuy HVAC maintenance article, with prioritized QA findings and a proposed WordPress release workflow.

Client / websiteFilterbuy HVAC Solutions
Project typeIndependent public-page audit and reoptimization recommendations
Audit dateOctober 2026
My roleSEO Specialist | Content QA & On-Page SEO Optimization

Please note: This is an independent portfolio assessment informed by my SEO work at Filterbuy (2023–2026). The suggestions are not represented as implemented or approved by Filterbuy, and no ranking or conversion improvements are claimed.

Project overview

The article, Central, Window & Outdoor AC Maintenance: Expert Tips to Keep Every System Running Smoothly (published April 13, 2026), is a homeowner education piece that also supports Filterbuy HVAC Solutions service inquiries. View the live article.

Article and SEO objectives

QA objective

Improve clarity, reduce repetition, verify claims and create a dependable WordPress release workflow.

What I prepared

An evidence-based editorial review, sample on-page rewrites and a ready-to-use publishing QA checklist.

Search focus

Answer the homeowner's maintenance questions directly, organized by AC type.

Existing content strengths

Separate guidance for each AC unit type, a seasonal checklist, useful government and trade sources, and an HVAC professional byline.

Six prioritized QA findings

Observations come from the public article. Priority is shown by label, not color alone.

High priority
1. Repetitive content and overlapping sections

Observed: The article includes Quick Answers, Top Takeaways, Final Thought, Next Steps and FAQs with overlapping maintenance guidance.

Proposed: Consolidate repeated advice into one quick-answer panel, one seasonal checklist and a concise closing CTA.

High priority
2. Factual claims requiring verification

Observed: Several quantified claims and service-call anecdotes are presented, including a 5-to-10-times repair-cost comparison.

Proposed: Verify against client records or primary sources; otherwise remove or qualify the exact claim.

High priority
3. DIY maintenance safety clarity

Observed: Coil rinsing and drain-line flushing are discussed alongside professional-only refrigerant and electrical work.

Proposed: Add power-off and manufacturer-instruction safeguards; route uncertain tasks to a qualified technician.

Medium priority
4. Content navigation and heading hierarchy

Observed: Central, window and outdoor tasks are followed by lengthy resources, statistics, next steps and FAQs.

Proposed: Use scannable jump links and keep maintenance-by-system sections together.

Medium priority
5. Image accessibility and alt text

Observed: The hero image and infographic have descriptive but lengthy, title-like image labels.

Proposed: Review alt text and captions for accurate visual meaning rather than repeated search phrases.

Medium priority
6. CTA consistency and link review

Observed: Service forms and calls to action appear in several areas, alongside outbound and internal links.

Proposed: Prioritize one main booking action, keep contextual links useful and test each destination before release.

Not independently verified: title tag, meta description, canonical, indexability, redirects, structured data, live link status and mobile behavior.

Proposed SEO improvements

Proposed SEO title

AC Maintenance: Central, Window & Outdoor Guide | Filterbuy

Proposed meta description

Maintain central, window and outdoor AC units with seasonal checklists, safe DIY tips and guidance on when to call an HVAC professional.

Keep the existing URL unless a redirect strategy is approved. Use keywords naturally and optimize for helpful answers, not a fixed word count or AI citation guarantee.

Before-and-after content example

Existing (observed)

The published article repeats maintenance guidance across five sections:

  • Quick Answers
  • Top Takeaways
  • Final Thought
  • Next Steps
  • FAQs
Proposed (not published)
How do you maintain central, window and outdoor AC units?

Start with the air filter, keep the condenser clear of debris, and monitor changes in cooling, noise and drainage. Window units also need clean reusable filters and tight seals. Follow the manufacturer’s instructions, switch off power before permitted cleaning, and leave electrical or refrigerant work to an HVAC technician.

Suggested article structure
  • H1 Central, Window & Outdoor AC Maintenance Guide
  • H2 Quick maintenance answers by AC type
  • H2 Central AC: filters, coils & drainage
  • H2 Window AC: filters, fins & window seals
  • H2 Outdoor condenser: debris, clearance & sound
  • H2 Seasonal checklist + when to call a pro
  • H2 Common questions + schedule service

WordPress publishing checklist

A practical release checklist, adapted from hands-on content operations for a human-reviewed, AI-assisted editorial workflow.

Editorial & fact check
  • Search intent and user questions answered directly
  • Stats and first-hand claims substantiated or softened
  • DIY guidance checked for safety and product nuance
  • Repeated sections trimmed; brand voice consistent
On-page SEO
  • Page title and meta description reviewed in the SEO plugin
  • One logical H1 with accessible H2/H3 hierarchy
  • Contextual internal links and citations reviewed
  • Image alt text serves accessibility and page meaning
WordPress pre-publish
  • Blocks, lists, tables and mobile preview checked
  • Jump links, buttons and booking forms tested
  • Featured image, slug, category and author verified
  • Canonical, indexability and relevant schema checked
After publish
  • Live URL tested on desktop and mobile
  • Source links and internal link targets checked
  • Changes logged with date and reviewer
  • GSC metrics reviewed later if account access exists

Verified claims and clear DIY boundaries build reader trust.

A reusable release process

A checklist that makes future refreshes consistent.

Featured Project

SEO & Content Work for an HVAC & Air Filtration Brand

A behind-the-scenes look at the SEO tasks I handled — and what I picked up about SEO, AEO, and GEO along the way.

IndustryHVAC / Air Filtration
RoleSEO Specialist — Content Operations
ScopeNational (US Market)
GoalConsistent Organic Growth

The Background

This project was for a national HVAC and air filtration brand, in a search landscape that looks simple on the surface — people search for filter sizes, replacement schedules, and brand comparisons. But more of those searches are now happening through AI assistants first. Someone asking "how often should I replace my MERV 13 filter?" might get an answer straight from ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Google's AI Mode, based on whichever sources those tools trust most.

That's the shift that makes this work matter for a business's bottom line: ranking on Google alone isn't enough anymore. Content also needs to be clear, well-structured, and credible enough for AI tools to cite it directly — because every AI citation is a chance to be recommended to a customer before they ever see a competitor's ad. My role covered the content and on-page side end-to-end: keyword research, writing and publishing, on-page optimization, and building the brand's presence across a few different sites and platforms so it earns trust in both traditional and AI search results.

What My Work Covered

Tasks Across Three Areas: SEO, AEO & GEO

I didn't design this framework — but my day-to-day tasks touched all three areas, and working across them taught me a lot.

SEO

Search Engine Optimization

The day-to-day work that helps pages show up in Google.

  • Keyword research across size-specific queries (e.g. "16x25x4 MERV 13 air filter"), replacement intent, and brand comparisons
  • Cross-linking filter size pages with MERV guides, symptom articles, and brand pages so they support each other
  • On-page optimization: title tags, meta descriptions, header hierarchy, and keyword placement
  • Adding FAQ and HowTo schema markup to help pages qualify for rich results
  • Writing articles for author profiles on two industry-focused editorial sites, to build topical authority beyond the brand's own domain
  • Helping manage the brand's Pinterest presence as part of its visual content strategy
AEO

Answer Engine Optimization

Writing content so it can be picked up in quick-answer boxes.

  • Wrote articles with a short, direct answer near the top (under 60 words) before going into more detail
  • Targeted People Also Ask questions: "what does MERV rating mean," "how to check if your air filter needs replacing," "what happens if you don't change your air filter"
  • Added FAQ schema to high-traffic informational pages to help with featured snippet and PAA placement
  • Wrote definitional/explainer content (e.g. what a MERV rating is) tied back to the brand's core topics
  • Used conversational phrasing suited to voice search and smart home assistants
GEO

Generative Engine Optimization

A newer area I got hands-on exposure to — helping content get picked up by AI tools.

  • Made sure articles had clear author bylines and were backed by specific, checkable facts — details that seem to help AI tools trust a source
  • Wrote and published articles across two industry-focused editorial sites, building a consistent, checkable track record on HVAC topics
  • Got into the habit of pairing the brand with specific, checkable product details and facts — something I noticed AI tools seem to pick up on
  • Saw firsthand that some of this content started showing up in Google AI Mode, Perplexity, and ChatGPT for target HVAC queries
  • Kept a consistent author identity across sites, which seems to support E-E-A-T (Google's trust signals)

What I Took Away

Why these three areas seem to go together

SEO

Good rankings still matter — but I noticed that when someone asks ChatGPT or Perplexity directly, a page can rank well and still never get clicked, because the AI just answers the question itself.

AEO

Writing content that's easy to pull into a featured snippet or quick-answer box helps with visibility — but on its own, it doesn't guarantee an AI tool will trust or cite it.

GEO

Getting cited by AI tools felt exciting, but most people are still searching the old-fashioned way too — so that traffic still matters just as much.

Together

Doing the SEO basics well, writing clear answer-first content, and staying consistent and well-attributed across sites seemed to support each other. I came away seeing these as connected tasks, not separate ones.

“

SEO gets you into the index. AEO can get you into the answer box. GEO might get you into the AI's reasoning. Working across all three on this project taught me they're really just different parts of the same job.

— What I took away from this project
